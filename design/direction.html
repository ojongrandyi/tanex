TANEX · DESIGN DIRECTION · TASK C.1 · 7 NOVEMBER 2026 · ENGLISH

One route, from your door to the world.

The idea is the route. A TanEx delivery is a line from one door to another, so the homepage is built as one: a single dashed orange route runs down the page, and a TanEx bike rides it through every section, from the door in the hero to the door in the footer.

Everything else stays quiet so that line can be seen: big bold type, generous space, calm motion, and the logo's three speed lines as the one decorative detail.

  • ChocolateCarries the weight: dark chapters, headlines, the main buttons in light sections.
  • CreamGives the air: light chapters, cards, room to read.
  • OrangeMarks the route and the next action only: buttons, the plane, route lines. Never body text on light.

2 · Type

Bold and upright, with one word that leans.

Plus Jakarta Sans 800 for headlines, tight and large. One word per headline leans in italic 800, like the slanted TanEx wordmark, led by the logo's three speed lines. Inter for reading, JetBrains Mono for anything a customer may need to copy: IDs, references, labels.

3 · Components

The same parts on cream and on chocolate.

Pill buttons, 10 px fields with labels above, 14 px cards with warm shadows. The dark versions are the same components with the role tokens switched, which is how C.2 will build them. Tab through the panels to see the focus ring.

Status chips use semantic colours (never brand colours) and always carry text. Status names from CONTENT §6.3.

4 · Homepage

The owner's order, with a lane for the bike.

Hero · Services · Bike delivery · Truck delivery · Global network · Smart labels · Get in touch · FAQ · Blog · Footer, with the English copy from CONTENT §2. Content and the bike never share space: the lane alternates right, left, right… so text never sits under the 3D bike. On phones the lane becomes a band between the text blocks.

5 · The Journey

Nine key frames, one bike, scrubbed by scroll.

One key frame per chapter (MOTION_3D_SPEC §6). Scroll lengths are in viewport heights; phones use about 60%. Each pinned chapter spends 15% arriving, 70% telling its story and 15% leaving. Only one camera move and one text reveal happen at a time, all on one ease. Captions are from CONTENT §2.10.

Model specification (MOTION_3D_SPEC §4)

  • Blender source + glTF 2.0 (meshopt or Draco), ≤ 8,000 triangles for bike + rider.
  • One material atlas, matte (no gloss, no chrome), colours only from the palette above.
  • Separate pivots: front wheel, rear wheel, handlebar with front fork (steers), rider torso (turns to glance at the phone holder), box lid.
  • Real-world scale in metres; origin on the ground between the wheels; facing +Z.
  • A small phone holder on the handlebar (chapter 2 shows a map with a moving dot on it).

Truck, same style

  • Light box truck: cream body, chocolate cab, an orange stripe, the TanEx wordmark on both sides (from the logo file, never redrawn).
  • Wheels as separate meshes; the cargo door opens for the hand-off in chapter 3.

These drawings are flat 2D guides for proportions and colour, not the final 3D look. The flyer's computer-generated images are used only as a style reference, never on the site (tracker #121).

For approval

What you are approving.

Nothing else gets built until you approve or ask for changes. After approval: tokens (C.2), core components (C.3), then the real homepage layout (E.1) and the Journey (phase H).

Decisions in this direction

  1. Palette and role tokens as in section 1, with the contrast rules.
  2. Type: Plus Jakarta Sans 800 headlines with one italic word led by three speed lines; Inter body; JetBrains Mono for IDs.
  3. Emphasis is never orange text. The italic word stays chocolate (light) or cream (dark); orange is kept for actions and the route.
  4. Chapter rhythm: dark hero · cream services · dark in-town · cream city-to-city · dark world · cream label, contact, FAQ, blog · dark footer.
  5. Bike lane alternates: right, left, right, left, right, left, right; none in FAQ and blog; a band between text on phones.
  6. Homepage headlines use the H1 size (they stay H2 in the page structure) so each chapter reads like a poster; the bike chapter and the footer arrival use Display.
  7. Header "Get a Quote" is an outline button over the dark hero (so the hero shows only two orange buttons: Track and Get a Quote) and turns orange once the header turns solid.
  8. The bike and rider look in section 6, including the proposed helmet and bike body colours.
